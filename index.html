Curious how it works?

The tech, in plain words.

A handful of free tools, wired together. Tap any term to expand — only if you want the detail.

What is GitHub, and why is it needed?

A website that stores the project's files and also runs it automatically on a free schedule — so there's no server to rent and no laptop to leave on. That's a big reason it costs nothing to run.

What is RSS?

A tidy, machine-readable list of a news site's latest articles. News sites publish it so software can read their headlines without a human clicking around. The tool reads these to gather each day's news.

What does the AI (and its “API”) do?

The AI is the brain that reads the headlines and writes the summary. The “API” is simply the doorway our program uses to send the headlines to the AI and get the finished brief back — over the internet. A secret “key” tells Google the request is ours, on the free tier.

How does it send the email?

Through a standard email method called SMTP, using a free Gmail account. The AI writes the brief in plain text; our code turns it into a tidy, formatted email and delivers it to everyone on the list, automatically.

Is it really free? Is it safe?

Yes — it runs entirely on free tiers. Any passwords or keys are stored encrypted, never inside the code, and the tool only reads public news.

Good to know

Frequently Asked Questions.

Tap any question to expand.

How much does it cost to run?

Nothing. It runs entirely on free tiers — GitHub for scheduling, Gemini's free AI tier, and Gmail. The only paid option (a stronger AI model) was deliberately left out to keep it free.

Is the data safe? Where do the passwords live?

The AI key and email password are stored as encrypted secrets in GitHub — never written in the code and never shown in logs. The tool only reads public news headlines; it doesn't touch sensitive internal data.

Could it cover other countries or topics?

Easily. Point it at different news sources and adjust the AI's instructions, and the same tool becomes a brief on any market or topic. That's exactly why the code is shared.

How accurate is the AI? Could it make things up?

The brief is a helpful first read, not gospel — every email says so and links back to the original articles. The AI is told to separate fact from analysis and not invent specifics, and a full log of what it was given and wrote is attached to every email for checking.

For other teams

Build your own version.

It's designed to be copied and pointed at any market — just change the news sources. You'll need three free accounts and three secret keys.

1

Copy the project

Fork the public code to get your own copy, then swap in your market's news sources and your team's email addresses.

2

Get a free Gemini AI key

At aistudio.google.com, create an API key in a project with no billing enabled, so it stays on the genuine free tier.

3

Set up a sending Gmail

Turn on 2-Step Verification, then create a 16-character “App Password” — a normal password won't work for a script.

4

Store three secrets in GitHub

Add the AI key, the Gmail address, and the App Password under Settings → Secrets. They're stored encrypted and never appear in the code.

5

Test it, then let it run

Trigger it once from the Actions tab to confirm the email arrives — after that, it runs itself on the daily schedule.
